# R4 — what tests would actually prove this aeroplane is agile?

## Decision and scope

Evaluate the full-size VX-J1, not the former RC model. The first defensible claim is **competitive low-subsonic, trimmed turn performance**, not superiority to every piloted aircraft. No computation in this memo establishes departure resistance or flight-control safety.

## Candidate performance target — not the first execution step

At ISA sea level, clean configuration, 11,006 kg, and 54.02 m² reference area, generate a trimmed drag polar and evaluate a **4 g sustained level turn at 120 m/s true airspeed**. These are proposed test conditions, not demonstrated capabilities. With g = 9.80665 m/s², the required lift coefficient is 4mg/(0.5 × 1.225 × 120² × S) = **0.906**; the geometrical turn rate is g√(4²−1)/120 = **18.13 deg/s**. The turn is sustained only if installed net thrust equals or exceeds trimmed drag, and lift, trim and structural limits all permit it. Failure of any condition falsifies this particular performance target; it does not prove the aircraft cannot be agile elsewhere.

**Status: needs a stated assumption.** Installed thrust versus speed and altitude, allowable load factor, and usable maximum lift are not established by the panel method. The 129 kN engine rating is not an installed flight thrust map. Do not call the resulting rate a prediction until those inputs have defensible bounds.

A measured roll-rate requirement must specify airspeed, load factor, initial state, actuator limits and the interval used to measure rate. A static rolling moment is not a roll rate; no numerical roll-rate capability is claimed here.
